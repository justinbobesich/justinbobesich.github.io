01 · The Idea

Why I wanted to build it

I loved arcade claw machines as a kid and wanted to build one of my own. The whole machine was my own idea. I planned it in my head instead of following a kit or a set of plans, and it became one of the largest projects I’ve taken on. I started it at about age 12.

Getting the inside to work took a lot of trial and error. Whenever a part failed or didn’t behave the way I intended, I changed it or replaced it and tested again. My first cabinet never fully worked, so I started over and built a second one, which is the machine I still have.

03 · How It Works

Coin to prize

CoinA coin starts the game
→
TimerOne minute to play
→
MoveJoystick moves the claw on a belt-driven gantry
→
Grab & dropClaw picks up a prize and releases it over the chute

The machine runs entirely on mechanical and electrical components, with no computer or Raspberry Pi inside. I bought the motors and other components, designed and 3D-printed my own pieces for the claw, joystick and buttons, and worked out how to put everything together.

The gantry. The claw hangs from a gantry that moves on two axes. Each axis runs on 7/16-inch hardwood dowel rails, with ball bearings rolling along them and 3D-printed carriages holding it all together. Toothed timing belts pull the carriages back and forth.

The claw. The claw is round and 3D-printed, with a small geared motor inside, and it hangs from the gantry on a coiled cable.

The controls. The control panel has a coin slot, a red three-digit display, a power switch, Grab and Drop buttons, two arrow buttons and a joystick with an X-shaped gate. Under the panel, the joystick is built from lever micro switches held in 3D-printed brackets.

The electronics. DC motors drive the gantry axes and the claw. The joystick and buttons use lever micro switches, and the red three-digit display on the panel is a digital timer relay module: when a coin goes in, it counts down the minute and cuts off the controls when time runs out. Blue LED strips light the cabinet.

05 · First Cabinet

The first attempt · 2022

This short video from April 2022, when I was 12, is the only record of my first cabinet. It shows a wooden frame with a base section at the front and a tall back panel.

The first cabinet never became fully functional. So I discarded it and built a new cabinet from scratch.

The first cabinet was an open wooden frame. For the second one I built a complete, closed cabinet with the prize chute, the control panel and the lighting designed in from the start.

06 · Second Cabinet

The final build · 2022–2023

The second cabinet is the machine I still have. By November 2022 it was built and lit, and by Christmas it was playing full games, like the one in the video at the top of this page. It still had an open front at that stage.

The video above shows an earlier claw design I tested in November 2022. I replaced it with the round 3D-printed claw used in the finished machine.

Improving it · spring 2023

Once it was working, I kept improving it. In spring 2023 I reworked the control panel and rebuilt the joystick from micro switches in 3D-printed brackets, one per direction, wired with spade connectors. The last step was the glass, which was ordered and cut to fit the cabinet.

The rework gave each joystick direction its own switch, mounted solidly in a printed bracket, and tidied the wiring under the panel so it would hold up to being played.

07 · The Machine Today

The finished machine

The finished machine has a glass front, blue lighting around the top and a prize chute. I still have it, and it still works.

08 · What I Learned

What I took away from it

Building, troubleshooting and rebuilding this machine taught me a lot. Most of the inside came from trying something, seeing where it failed and changing it. That’s still how I approach projects now.

A few examples: the first cabinet never fully worked, so I started over. The claw went through more than one design before the round one. The joystick was rebuilt from individual micro switches in printed brackets. Each change came from something that wasn’t working well enough.
